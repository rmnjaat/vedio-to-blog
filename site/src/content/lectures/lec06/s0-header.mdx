### The social network so far

In Lecture 7 (coming soon), Instagram got a working photo pipeline. A photo goes from the user’s phone straight to S3 with a pre-signed URL, never through the API servers. The post that shares it is stored apart from the image, and whatever can be derived, such as the image’s path, is not stored. Photos are served through a CDN on its own subdomain, which pulls from an origin on a miss, keeps a copy with a TTL, and can resize or convert an image from the query parameters in its URL. Counting hashtags runs on Kafka.

This lecture keeps that toolbox and builds four smaller features on top of it. Chapter 1 opens the CDN’s black box and writes the image handler behind it yourself. Chapters 2 and 3 use that handler for Gravatar, reuse the upload service unchanged, and make the CDN’s resizing your own code. Chapter 4 tags people in those photos, and Chapters 5 and 6 build the badge on the messages icon, which ends at the auxiliary-database pattern.
