### Nine lines to keep [[rail: Heuristics]]

The same few habits did the work in both builds, whatever box was on the board:

1. Find the core property and access pattern first; which tool fits comes second, never your affinity for one ([§1.2](#s1-2)). [[L-0029]]
2. Estimate before you optimise, and ask whether the problem is storage or computation. Eight gigabytes was never the issue; six million writes a minute were ([§2.6](#s2-6), [§4.1](#s4-1)). At every step ask “can I change this, is there an alternative?”, and write down every option you considered and why you took your path ([§2.6](#s2-6)). [[L-0105 L-0059 L-0060]]
3. Don’t add moving parts. Never draw a box you wouldn’t want to build, run and monitor; if the database can expire entries natively, offload it instead of running your own deleter ([§2.7](#s2-7), [§2.8](#s2-8)). [[L-0067 L-0068]]
4. Know how a feature works inside, not just that it exists: AOF and RDB, how to configure them, how often they flush ([§3.2](#s3-2)). [[L-0086]]
5. Pool your connections, with a budget. A pool is always a great option for many micro updates, and a blindly configured one caps how many servers you can run ([§4.3](#s4-3), [§4.7](#s4-7)). [[L-0131 L-0320]]
6. Start conservative, observe the metrics, then retune ([§4.5](#s4-5)). [[L-0161]]
7. Ask of every table: does it need recoverability, archival or audit? If yes, soft delete ([§5.2](#s5-2)). [[L-0183]]
8. Keep rows to a bare minimum: very large text as long text, everything else short ([§5.6](#s5-6)). [[L-0199]]
9. A cache is anything that saves an expensive disk I/O, network I/O or computation, not a place in RAM ([§6.1](#s6-1)). [[L-0244]]

And the habit behind all nine: implement the core of each system yourself, over the next year or so. Implementing it is what shows you where a design falters. [[L-0009]]

### Trade-offs, always [[rail: Trade-offs]]

The whole domain is full of trade-offs. There is no one right answer to anything; even 2 plus 2 can be reformulated. Every time you use something, a store, a cache, a pool, you gain something and you lose something, so stay open to the trade-off. [[L-0258]]

That intuition is built over the eight weeks. Every system the course designs will be argued for hours, and from next week the brainstorming won’t move forward until there is an answer to why a decision is made and what it trades away. [[L-0328]] Keep asking questions along the way: they surface the cases a lecture would otherwise miss ([§6.4](#s6-4)). [[L-0281]]

Where to stop overthinking gets its own session later: the same system seen through the eyes of a manager, a product manager, a senior engineer and a junior engineer, the “hats”.
