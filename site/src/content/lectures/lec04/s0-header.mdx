This is the first session of the course: eight weeks of system design aimed at building raw intuition for systems, always from the implementation side rather than just drawing boxes. Drawing boxes is something “you can do as a kid also”; going into the intricate details is what makes an engineer better. [[L-0001]]

The course does not prepare you for interviews. Its goals are the joy of engineering and the intuition to build any system in the world, from first principles, with simple designs that scale the way real systems are built. Literally every system in the course can be implemented on a local machine; you need no fancy infrastructure. Over the next year or so, implement the core of each one (the flash-sale module itself, not its UI or every CRUD API). The devil lies in the details: implementing shows where a design falters, and real teams expect engineers to implement, not just draw boxes. [[L-0003 L-0008 L-0009 L-0010]]

Today has two parts. First, an online/offline indicator like the one on Facebook or Twitter; then, after the break, how to approach system design, with databases and caching worked through on a Medium-like blogging app. Since it is day one, this page keeps things simple and puts little focus on scale: performance still comes up, but scale arrives in weeks two to four. The first week is foundations only, so everyone starts level; brainstorming whole systems begins in the second week. It opens with a build rather than theory on purpose: starting from the foundations used to become too theoretical. [[L-0019 L-0020 L-0016 L-0167]]

**How to use this page.** The course is built on live brainstorming, which trains you to think about systems intuitively rather than in question-and-answer form, so stop at every pause-and-think prompt and answer it before you open it. You will meet new terms; this is an intermediate course, so note them down and read about them yourself. Revise: the weeks chain together, and by week 7 a flash sale takes about ten minutes to design. The pre-reads on Redis and DynamoDB are curated for this session and cover most of what you need.
