### Key takeaways, and the habits behind them [[rail: Heuristics]]

The board’s own list for the hashtag design was short: Kafka as a glue, the adapter pattern, effective batching and counting, and read and write path optimizations. [[L-0289 L-0290]] Behind them, and behind the photo feature, the same habits kept coming back:

1. A CDN sits transparently between your user and your origin; it serves what it has and fetches the rest ([2](#s2)). [[L-0018]]
2. Keep it simple. Complex systems are easy to build; simple ones are hard, because engineers are trained to think in a complex way ([3.2](#s3-2)). [[L-0067]]
3. System design is building blocks: once you know how a solution works, you can copy it anywhere. GitHub’s attachment upload and Instagram’s photo upload are one and the same ([3.4](#s3-4)). [[L-0087]]
4. If something is derivable, don’t store it: store the crux and derive the rest when you need it ([4.1](#s4-1)). [[L-0125]]
5. Structure the system so you don’t bind yourself to any one vendor or provider ([4.2](#s4-2)). [[L-0130]]
6. Privacy is enforced in the API server, which alone can see who follows whom; the CDN never can ([5.3](#s5-3)). [[L-0172]]
7. Reuse what already exists rather than reinventing the wheel every time ([6.2](#s6-2)). [[L-0184]]
8. Drawing boxes is the easiest part; the nitty-gritty of the implementation at every step is what matters ([6.3](#s6-3)). [[L-0152]]
9. Design the storage from the page you must render: what do you need to store to render it? ([7.2](#s7-2)) [[L-0215]]
10. When you count something in huge quantities, batch: whenever you can batch, batch ([8.4](#s8-4)). [[L-0252]]
11. Decouple as much as you can: what doesn’t need to be synchronous shouldn’t be ([9.1](#s9-1)). [[L-0307]]
12. Understand your read paths and write paths, and tackle one at a time ([9.3](#s9-3)). [[L-0277]]
