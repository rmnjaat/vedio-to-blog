# Lecture 04 (lec02): animation plan

Selection follows `.claude/skills/explainer-animation/references/choosing.md`. A concept gets a clip only if the change over time *is* the lesson. There are 3–5 clips per lecture, at most one per top-level section, and never two clips without prose between them. This lecture gets four: §3, §7, §8 and §9, each in a different chapter page. Each clip below is a shortlist entry plus beats; the full `storyboard.yaml` / `storyboard.md` are written at build time. Canvas 1200×800, 30 fps. Marker colours as in `figures.md`: blue = request/data path, green = ack/saved/delivered, red = failure/stale/limit/lost, graphite otherwise. Past states ghost at 38%. Glyphs are the ones in `figures.md` §0 (edge server with connection slots, WS line, `tcp` link, real-time Pub/Sub pipe with dotted lanes, `kafka` pipe with message squares). Frame 0 equals the named static twin; the last frame is the poster.

**Phone (all clips):** every clip also gets a portrait layout, 800×1200 at 30 fps, rendered as a separate composition (`<id>-phone`) with the same timeline. Lanes that run left → right on desktop stack top → bottom, captions sit in a band at the bottom, labels stay ≥ 12 px when the player is 358 px wide (mono 26 units, hand 28 units at 800 wide). Each clip says how its parts move.

## 1. `l02-column-scan`: one average, two layouts (§3.2, about 28 s)
- **Mechanism:** the board's query `SELECT avg(price) WHERE ts = '___';` runs on the same `stock_ticks` data stored two ways. Top lane, row store: a blue read head moves strip by strip through the rows; each strip is read whole (all five cells light), then only `price` and `ts` are kept and the other three cells flash red. A counter runs up `values read`. Bottom lane, column store: only `price.col` and `ts.col` light blue and are read top to bottom; the other three files stay grey with hand `no IO`. Last, the table widens to the board's 100 columns, and two bars compare what each layout reads: 100 of 100 vs 2 of 100.
- **Why motion beats a static figure:** the lesson is how much each layout has to *touch* to answer one question, and that the row store can't skip. Watching the head pass through every column of every row, while the other lane opens two files and is done, makes the cost visible as work over time. Fig 3·a shows the layouts but not the read.
- **Static twin:** Fig 3·a (frame 0 is Fig 3·a with the query chip added); board D-01 (`b_001845.jpg`, the query and "discarding 98 of them").
- **Ledger taught:** L-0053, L-0056, L-0057, L-0058, L-0059, L-0062. **Context only:** L-0054, L-0060, L-0071 (the named-columns question is P1 and the prose), L-0066 (the rule follows in 3.3).
- **Fidelity notes:** 10,000 rows is the class's number (L-0057). The counter (`50,000` vs `20,000` values) is arithmetic on 10,000 rows × 5 or × 2 columns, marked illustrative; it counts values, not bytes or disk pages, and ignores compression (that is 3.4). The 100-column bars and "discarding 98 of them" are the board's (D-01, S15). The row store is the class's model, a full scan with no index; the clip says nothing about indexes. "Thousands of columns" (L-0060) is not on screen, per the fact-check note (wide tables exist but are not typical).
- **Beats:**
  - B0 (0–4 s) hold: Fig 3·a, with the query chip mono `SELECT avg(price) WHERE ts = '___'` above both lanes; hand `10,000 rows`. *"One query: one day's average price. Same table, two layouts."*
  - B1 (4–11 s) row lane: the read head sweeps strip after strip (six drawn, then an ellipsis that fast-forwards), each strip lit whole; counter mono `values read: 50,000`. *"Row store: it reads every row whole, then keeps price and timestamp."*
  - B2 (11–15 s) the `symbol`, `name`, `exchange` cells of every read strip flash red; hand `can't skip: where does the row end?` *"Symbol, name, exchange: read for nothing. It can't skip them."*
  - B3 (15–20 s) column lane: `price.col` then `ts.col` light blue and are read down; the other three files stay grey, hand `no IO`; counter mono `values read: 20,000`. *"Column store: two files, price and ts. The other three: no IO at all."*
  - B4 (20–25 s) both lanes shrink to two bars; the header widens to hand `100 columns`; row bar fills 100/100 (red hand `discarding 98 of them`), column bar 2/100 (blue). *"With 100 columns: the row store reads all 100, the column store 2."*
  - B5 (25–28 s, hold) poster: the two bars under the query chip. *"The query decides the layout."*
- **Phone:** the two lanes stack (row store on top, column store under it); the five column files become a 3 + 2 grid as in Fig 3·b's phone layout; the counters sit at each lane's right edge, the bars full-width in B4.
- **Predict line (before the clip, after P1):** "Five columns, 10,000 rows. How much does each layout read to answer one average?"
- **After:** "Naming the columns in `SELECT` doesn't change what a row store reads; storing them apart does (3.3)."

## 2. `l02-three-ways-to-persist`: when "saved" and "shown" happen (§7.3, about 34 s)
- **Mechanism:** one message, mono `standup in 5`, sent by A to B three ways on three lanes with a shared time axis. Each lane ends at B's screen and has two marks, `saved` and `shown`, that tick when they happen. ① over HTTP: `lb` → `api` → shard (green `saved`), then `api` → edge server → B (`shown`). ② over the WS line to the edge server → B at once (`shown`); the edge server drops the square into `kafka`, where it waits; a clock beside the worker reads hand `lag: 10 min`. ③ over WS to the edge server → B (`shown`); nothing else. Then B refreshes 10 s after the send on every lane: ① reloads it from the database; ② the bubble disappears (red, not in the database yet); ③ there is nothing to load. Last, the axis jumps past the lag: the worker pulls the square, writes it (green `saved`), and ②'s refresh now shows it.
- **Why motion beats a static figure:** the three paths differ in the *order* of two events, and the failure the class warned about (a refresh hiding a message you already saw) exists only inside the lag window. Fig 7·c shows the routes; only time shows that ② shows before it saves and that a refresh inside the window disagrees with the screen.
- **Static twin:** Fig 7·c (frame 0 is Fig 7·c's phone strips laid side by side, graphite) and T4; board D-04 (`b_022045.jpg`).
- **Ledger taught:** L-0206, L-0209, L-0214, L-0218, L-0220, L-0389, L-0390. **Context only:** L-0215 (the device copy, in prose), L-0219 and L-0222 (the RuleBox after the clip), L-0221, L-0318.
- **Fidelity notes:** lane labels keep the class's hedges: `① Slack (instructor's hypothesis)`, `② WhatsApp-like (hypothetical)`, `③ Zoom chat (default)`. Slack's published path (A-MLA-03) fans out through channel and gateway servers and does not say the write completes first, so the clip doesn't claim Slack's internals; the persist-then-broadcast order is the class's type 1 (L-0206). ③ "never saved" is the default in-meeting chat (A-MLA-12, continuous meeting chat persists). The 10-minute lag is the class's example (L-0218); the 10 s refresh moment is illustrative (outline), and the axis is broken between 10 s and 10 min for legibility (hand `≈`). The ② refresh is the class's "why not for Slack" scenario: a client that reads the channel back from the database. The hand label on it says `a Slack-style refresh reads the DB`, because a WhatsApp client keeps its device copy (L-0215). Board colours (white/green/blue) are not used; paths are numbered.
- **Beats:**
  - B0 (0–3.5 s) hold: three lanes ①②③, A at the left of each, B's screen at the right, empty `saved` / `shown` marks. *"One message, sent three ways. Watch saved and shown."*
  - B1 (3.5–10 s) lane ①: blue A → `lb` → `api` → shard, green `saved`; then `api` → edge → B, `shown`. *"① Over HTTP: the API server saves it, then the edge server shows it."*
  - B2 (10–16 s) lane ②: blue A → edge over WS → B, `shown` at once; the square drops into `kafka` and waits; the worker's clock `lag: 10 min`. *"② Over the WebSocket: shown at once. Kafka holds it; a worker saves it later."*
  - B3 (16–20.5 s) lane ③: blue A → edge → B, `shown`; the `saved` mark stays empty, hand `never saved`. *"③ Shown to whoever is connected now. Never saved."*
  - B4 (20.5–28 s) playhead at hand `+10 s`; B refreshes on all three lanes: ① reloads with a green tick; ② the bubble vanishes, red hand `not in the DB yet`; ③ empty, hand `nothing to load`. *"B refreshes 10 s later. ① It's there. ② It vanished. ③ Nothing to load."*
  - B5 (28–34 s, ends in a ≥ 2.5 s hold) the axis jumps `≈ 10 min`; the worker pulls the square (dashed, worker → `kafka`), writes it, green `saved`; ② refresh shows it. Poster: the three lanes annotated `saved → shown` · `shown → saved` · `shown`. *"After the lag, ② is saved too: delivered now, stored late."*
- **Phone:** the three lanes stack top to bottom, each a single row `A → … → B` with its parts shrunk to the Fig 7·c phone strips; the time axis becomes a playhead chip (`+0 s`, `+10 s`, `≈ 10 min`) in the top-right corner.
- **Predict line (before the clip, after P5):** "Persistence lags ten minutes. On which path does a refresh hide a message you already saw?"
- **After:** "Different guarantees, different architecture" → RuleBox §7.

## 3. `l02-mesh-to-pubsub`: links grow, then collapse into one pipe (§8.4, about 33 s)
- **Mechanism:** edge servers arrive one at a time and each must open a `tcp` link to every server already there. A counter tracks the links (1, 3, 6, 10), and WS1's slot bar (Fig 8·c) gains one red peer slot per new server after its four blue user slots. With five servers the mesh is drawn; then every link is erased, a real-time Pub/Sub pipe draws in the middle, each server draws one elbow into it, and WS1's bar drops to four users plus one graphite link. A message from A shows the new path: WS1 publishes once, the pipe pushes it to the other servers and holds nothing.
- **Why motion beats a static figure:** the mesh's cost is a *growth rate*: each new server costs one link per existing server, and every one of those links eats a slot on every server. Watching the count and the red part of the bar climb as servers arrive, then fall to one, is the lesson; Fig 8·b step 3 and Fig 8·c show only the four-server state.
- **Static twin:** Fig 8·b step 3 + Fig 8·c (frame 0 is Fig 8·b step 1's end state); end state ≈ Fig 8·d with five servers; boards D-05 (`b_020445.jpg`), D-06 (`b_021900.jpg`), D-13 (`b_023009.jpg`).
- **Ledger taught:** L-0265, L-0276, L-0277, L-0278, L-0280, L-0281, L-0282, L-0283. **Context only:** L-0242 (the toy limit), L-0258, L-0261, L-0263, L-0284 (no delivery guarantee: that is clip 4's job).
- **Fidelity notes:** 4 users per server is the toy limit (L-0242; hand `toy limit` on the bar). The 7 connections, 3 of them for other servers, are the class's numbers for four servers (L-0276). The fifth server, `10 links` and WS1's `4 + 4` bar are arithmetic from L-0265 (every server to every other), marked illustrative; they answer the predict line. No claim about ports: the limit is drawn as slots only (SN-06 corrects the ports claim in prose). The push goes to every connected server here, as 8.4 teaches it (L-0283); per-channel subscriptions come in 8.5 and are not shown. Redis is labelled `real-time pub/sub (redis)`; no throughput or latency numbers appear.
- **Beats:**
  - B0 (0–3.5 s) hold: `ws1` (A–D) —`tcp`— `ws2` (E); counter mono `links: 1`; WS1's bar `A B C D` blue + 1 red. *"Two servers, one TCP link. WS1: four users plus one link."*
  - B1 (3.5–9 s) `ws3` (F) drops in; links draw to `ws1`, then `ws2`; counter `links: 3`; WS1's bar gains a red slot. *"A third server must link to both others: 3 links."*
  - B2 (9–15 s) `ws4` arrives; three links draw one by one; counter `links: 6`; WS1's bar `4 + 3`, red bracket hand `3 serve no user`. *"Four servers: 6 links. 3 of WS1's 7 connections serve no user."*
  - B3 (15–21 s) `ws5` arrives (hand `illustrative`); four more links; counter `links: 10`; WS1's bar `4 + 4`; hand `every server ↔ every other`. *"A fifth server needs 4 more links. Every server links to every other."*
  - B4 (21–28 s) all `tcp` links erase together; the pipe draws in the centre, mono `redis`, hand `real-time pub/sub`; one elbow per server, one at a time; counter `links: 5`, hand `one per server`; WS1's bar `4 + 1`, the extra slot graphite. *"Put a real-time Pub/Sub in the middle: one link per server."*
  - B5 (28–33 s, ends in a ≥ 2.5 s hold) A sends (blue square) → `ws1` → B, C, D; `ws1` → pipe; the pipe pushes copies to `ws2`…`ws5` (heads at the servers); the pipe stays empty, hand `keeps nothing`. Poster. *"WS1 publishes once. The Pub/Sub pushes it on and keeps nothing."*
- **Phone:** the servers sit on a 2 × 3 grid (ws1 top-left), the counter and WS1's bar in a band under the grid; in B4 the pipe runs vertically through the grid's centre column, as in Fig 8·d's phone layout.
- **Predict line (before the clip, after P7 and the 8.4 opening):** "Add a fifth server. How many new links does the mesh need, and how many does the Pub/Sub need?"
- **After:** "Real-time, not Kafka: a Pub/Sub that pushes to whoever is subscribed right now and stores nothing" → Fig 8·d and T5.

## 4. `l02-missed-message`: lost in real time, fixed by a timer (§9.3, about 29 s)
- **Mechanism:** Fig 9·b's message path in a reduced form: A on `ws1`, E on `ws2`, the `redis pub/sub` pipe between the two servers, and below them `kafka` → `worker` → messages `db`, plus an `api` box beside E. E's client has a small clock glyph. A posts in C1. The message is persisted as always (edge → `kafka`, worker pulls, `db` green `saved`). At the moment WS1 publishes, WS2's link to the pipe is broken: the pipe pushes to no one connected and the square fades out (red). WS2 reconnects, and nothing replays. Then E's clock fires: E's client → `api` mono `GET last 50 (C1)` → `db`, the list comes back, and the missing line appears on E's screen (green).
- **Why motion beats a static figure:** the loss happens only because the publish falls inside a short disconnection, a window in time, and the fix is a later timer event on the same axis. A still can show the parts but not that the message was stored all along while the screen stayed wrong until the timer ran.
- **Static twin:** Fig 9·b (frame 0 is its reduced real-time + persistence columns); board D-11 (`b_022945.jpg`, "What if some messages are not sent in realtime? ← Transient issue").
- **Ledger taught:** L-0284, L-0344, L-0379, L-0332, L-0334, L-0345. **Context only:** L-0282 (Pub/Sub keeps nothing, taught in 8.4), L-0333 (clicking the channel loads it too, in prose), L-0346 (WhatsApp's notice, unverifiable, prose only).
- **Fidelity notes:** the one-second blink is illustrative (outline's predict line); the class says only "not connected at that moment" (L-0284). The timer's interval was not given in class, so the clock shows no number. "Last 50" is the class's (L-0345). The persistence path is the type-2 path of the final diagram (L-0338); "always persisted" is shown as a green `saved`, and the idempotent-insert caveat (A-MLA-09) stays in prose. The board's second fix, buffering in another Kafka and replaying (L-0334 (2)), is named in the after-sentence, not drawn. Redis's at-most-once delivery (A-SRW-10) is in prose, not on screen. The "correction job" wording is the board's.
- **Beats:**
  - B0 (0–3 s) hold: A on `ws1`, E on `ws2`, the pipe between, the persistence column below; E's screen shows C1's last lines. *"A posts in C1. E is on WS2, across Redis."*
  - B1 (3–8 s) A → `ws1` (blue); `ws1` → `kafka` (dashed); worker → `kafka` hand `pull`; worker → `db`, green `saved`. *"The message is persisted through Kafka, as always."*
  - B2 (8–14 s) WS2's elbow into the pipe breaks (red dashed, hand `disconnected`); `ws1` → pipe mono `PUBLISH c1`; the square sits in the pipe, finds no `ws2`, fades with a red ring, hand `lost: Redis keeps nothing`. *"WS2 is disconnected right then. Redis pushes to no one, and keeps nothing."*
  - B3 (14–18 s) WS2's elbow redraws solid graphite; nothing arrives; a red `?` beside E's screen. *"WS2 is back, but nothing replays. E doesn't see it."*
  - B4 (18–25 s) E's clock ticks round; E's client → `api` (blue) mono `GET last 50 (C1)`; `api` → `db`; the list returns; the missing line `standup in 5` slides into place on E's screen, green tick; hand `correction job`. *"On a timer, E's client fetches the last 50 and renders what it missed."*
  - B5 (25–29 s, hold) poster: hand on the persistence column `guaranteed`, on the pipe `not guaranteed`. *"Persistence is guaranteed. The real-time push is not."*
- **Phone:** vertical: A / `ws1` on top, the pipe as a horizontal band in the middle, `ws2` / E under it; the persistence column (`kafka` → worker → `db`) and `api` move to a strip at the bottom; E's screen sits beside `ws2` at 60% width.
- **Predict line (before the clip):** "WS2 blinked for one second while A posted. When does E see the message?"
- **After:** "For an enterprise the stored copy is what matters; a timer, a click on the channel, or replaying a buffered Kafka covers the push that was lost."
